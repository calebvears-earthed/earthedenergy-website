The working
Show, do not tell

$600 a quarter is $2,400 a year. At 42 cents that is about 5,700 kWh, or 15.7 kWh a day. Divide by Adelaide's 4.5 peak sun hours and 365 days and you need roughly 3.5 kW of panels just to match consumption. We multiply by 1.6 for battery charging and winter, and land at 6.6 kW as the floor, often 10 kW if the roof allows.

For the battery: 15.7 kWh a day, and an evening-heavy household uses about 70 percent of that after the sun sets. That is 11 kWh, rounded up to a 15 kWh battery. Price it, deduct the rebates, divide by annual savings at 80 percent of the bill, and you have a payback. Our calculator runs exactly this maths on your numbers.

When the battery waits
The downside, named

On a $400 quarter there is only about $1,000 a year of evening power to displace. A 10 kWh battery costs around $8,000 after rebate. That is an eight-year payback on the battery alone, against four years for the panels. We quote solar-only with a hybrid inverter and tell you what the battery will cost to add when an EV or heat pump arrives.

On a $1,000 quarter the same battery displaces $2,500 a year and pays back in about three. Bigger bills make batteries look clever. Small bills make them look expensive. Neither is the battery's fault.
