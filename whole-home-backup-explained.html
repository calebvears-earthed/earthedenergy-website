Whole-home Backup Explained

Two architectures we install, what each one can and cannot start, and our one firm rule.

On this page

  1. GoodWe ESA vs Sigenergy + Gateway on backup

What the words mean

Whole-home vs essential circuits

Essential-circuits backup means we move four or five circuits (fridge, lights, a power circuit, the pump) onto a sub-board that the battery carries. Everything else goes dark. Cheaper, and enough for many homes.

Whole-home backup means the transfer switch sits ahead of the entire switchboard. Every circuit stays live, including ovens, aircon and the EV charger, limited only by the inverter's backup rating. It costs more in switchboard work and it needs an inverter that can actually start those loads.

The two architectures

A Sigenergy stack without the Gateway has no backup at all. It is a good daytime battery and a dark house in a blackout. People buy it expecting backup and find out in the first storm. So we do not sell it that way. Either you get whole-home backup properly with the Gateway, or you go GoodWe ESA and get the built-in version.

Which one? If your bill is under about $800 a quarter, you are single-phase and you do not need to start a big compressor on battery, GoodWe. If you are three-phase, planning an EV, or want the house to feel normal in a blackout, Sigenergy with Gateway. Full comparison in GoodWe vs Sigenergy .

Figures in these guides are indicative. The price, and any rebate, that applies to your house is the one on the written quote.
