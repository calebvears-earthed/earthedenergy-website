Knowledge · Battery sizing

Battery Sizing

Forty to seventy percent of what you use in a day, depending on when you use it.

The rule of thumb

kWh · daily consumption

Start with your daily kWh from the bill. If the house is empty until 5 pm and busy until midnight, about 70 percent of your consumption happens after the sun is useful, so the battery needs to hold 70 percent of a day. If someone is home all day running aircon and a pool pump, the panels cover that directly and the battery only needs the overnight tail, around 40 percent. Mixed households sit near 55.

A 20 kWh-a-day evening household lands at 14 kWh, which we round up to a 15 kWh battery. A 20 kWh-a-day daytime household lands at 8, rounded to 10. Both GoodWe Lynx and Sigenergy modules come in steps that make this easy.

EV · pool · heat pump

An EV charged at home adds 8 to 12 kWh a day. A pool pump adds 4 to 6 in summer. A heat pump hot water unit adds about 2 but removes a gas bill. If any of those are on the horizon, we either size the battery up now or, more often, spec a modular stack with spare slots so you add 5 or 8 kWh later without touching the inverter.

Modular is the reason we install GoodWe and Sigenergy rather than fixed-size units. See the batteries we install .

When bigger does not help

A 30 kWh battery on a 10 kW array in June will rarely see full charge. The array makes 18 kWh on a clear winter day and the house takes half of that directly. Oversizing the battery against the array wastes money on capacity that only fills in summer, when you need it least.

The pairing we see work most often in the Hills is roughly 1.5 to 2 kWh of battery per kW of solar: 10 kW with 15 to 20 kWh, 13 kW with 20 to 25 kWh. Backup requirements can push above that. Payback usually does not.

Figures in these guides are indicative. The price, and any rebate, that applies to your house is the one on the written quote.
