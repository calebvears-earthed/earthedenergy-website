Monitoring Apps

What each app shows, what to check weekly, and what we watch from our end.

On this page

  2. Did the panels produce today

SEMS Portal · iOS and Android

The home screen is a live flow diagram: panels to house, house to battery, battery to grid, updated every few seconds. Below it, today's totals in kWh and dollars at the tariff we enter at commissioning. Historical views by day, month and year, and a battery tab showing state of charge and cycles.

We set up the account on install day, add your tariff, and we hold installer access so we can see faults before you do. Wi-Fi and 4G both supported, lifetime access, no subscription.

mySigen · iOS and Android

mySigen shows the same live flow plus module-level battery data, EV charging sessions if you have the integrated charger, and a scheduling engine that learns your usage and weather forecast to decide when to charge and discharge. You can set backup reserve, so the battery always holds, say, 30 percent for a blackout.

Firmware updates arrive over the air. We get fault alerts at our end and usually call you before you have noticed anything.

What to check

Three things, once a week, thirty seconds

Did the panels produce today

On a clear day in January a 10 kW array should show 35 kWh or more. In June, 15 to 20. A clear day showing under half that means a string is down or something is shading. Call us.

Did the battery fill

State of charge should hit 90 to 100 percent by mid-afternoon most days from September to April. If it stops at 60 for a week, either the array is underperforming or your daytime loads grew.

Grid import overnight

Should be close to zero from October to March. Some import in winter is normal. If you are importing 5 kWh every night in summer, something has changed and we want to know.
