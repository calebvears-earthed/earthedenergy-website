Cheaper Home Batteries

The federal Cheaper Home Batteries discount is paid as small-scale technology certificates (STCs). It comes to approximately 30% of the eligible battery cost. The exact discount varies with usable capacity and the installation date, and the rate steps down from 1 January 2027. We calculate it on your written quote. We do not publish a fixed dollar amount.

An STC represents one megawatt hour of clean generation. Your system is credited with the certificates it is expected to produce over the deeming period, which drops by a year every January until the scheme ends in 2030. For 2026 that is five years. Zone 3 (Adelaide and the Hills) carries a rating of 1.382 MWh per kW per year.

So a 10 kW system earns 10 × 1.382 × 5 = 69 certificates. At a spot price around $39 that is roughly $2,700, or about $310 per kW after the registry agent takes their cut. It comes off the quote as a point-of-sale discount. You never see the certificates.

SA retailers · 2026

The feed-in tariff is not a rebate, it is a retail rate, and it is low. Most SA retailers pay between 3 and 8 cents per kWh exported in 2026, against a blended import rate near 42 cents. Every kilowatt hour you use yourself is worth five to ten times one you export.

That single fact drives most of our design advice: soak up your own production with hot water, aircon and a battery before you export. See reading your power bill to find your own rates.

Figures in these guides are indicative. The price, and any rebate, that applies to your house is the one on the written quote.
