Scenario A · the single-tenant daytime build

Small commercial · single tenant

Dog groomer, hair salon, cafe, workshop, office. Trades 7 am to 5 pm, six days a week. Bill north of $500 a quarter. 30 kW solar, no battery.

  • 30 kW solar · no battery
  • 63 × Trina Vertex S+ 475W · 25-yr warranty
  • 2 × GoodWe MT Series 15 kW 3-phase · 10-yr warranty
  • SAPN flexible export commercial approval
  • Optional expansion port for future battery
  • No CHB rebate (solar only)
  • 2-week install lead time

Perfect for a business that trades daylight hours.

Thirty kilowatts on the roof covers around 45,000 kWh a year of solar production. If your business runs 7 am to 5 pm six days a week (dog groomer, hair salon, cafe, workshop, office, dental clinic, small showroom), you will self-consume around 70 to 80% of that generation directly during trading hours. The rest exports for a small feed-in credit.

A $2,000-a-quarter business bill typically drops to under $500. Payback lands in 2 to 3 years on the numbers we have modelled for similar-scale jobs. After payback, that is $6,000 to $7,000 of free power a year for twenty-plus years.

Solar-only makes sense for daytime businesses. Adding a battery only pays off if you have significant after-hours load or want blackout backup for critical equipment.

Scenario B · the warehouse or industrial build

Larger warehouse or industrial

Three-phase supply, 30 to 100 kW load, single roof span. 99 kW solar, 100 kWh battery.

  • 99 kW solar · 100 kWh battery
  • 209 × Trina Vertex S+ 475W · 25-yr warranty
  • 2 × Sigenergy SigenStor 50 kW 3-phase (paralleled) · 12-yr warranty
  • 100 kWh usable · SigenStor BAT modular · 15-yr warranty
  • Sigen Gateway TP · whole-site 3-phase backup
  • Right at the 100 kW STC-eligibility threshold
  • LGC-eligible for solar over 100 kW (larger builds)
  • 4-week install lead time

The biggest system that still claims the small-scale STC rebate.

Ninety-nine kilowatts of solar sits right at the ceiling of the small-scale renewable scheme. Above 100 kW, you cross into the Large-scale Generation Certificate framework, which pays differently and has different admin overhead. Below 100 kW, you keep the STC rebate applied up front: around $27,000 off the delivered price on this size build.

The 100 kWh battery covers night-shift equipment, blackout backup, and load-shifting away from peak-tariff windows. Payback across the whole system: 4 to 5 years. After that, a $10,000 to $15,000 quarterly bill drops to a few hundred dollars for twenty-plus years.

Above 100 kW we design and quote as an LGC-scheme project · different rebate mechanics, still competitive. Utility-grade design experience from our HV division (111 MW BESS operations, 5 MW solar farm) applies directly.

Scenario C · the multi-tenant build

Multi-tenant commercial

Shared-meter buildings where the landlord and tenants want to split cost or ownership. Embedded network or per-tenant metering, one system.

  • 60 kW solar · 50 kWh battery · shared
  • 126 × Trina Vertex S+ 475W · 25-yr warranty
  • 2 × GoodWe MT Series 30 kW 3-phase · 10-yr warranty
  • 50 kWh usable · GoodWe Lynx C commercial · 10-yr warranty
  • Per-tenant sub-metering and billing portal
  • Landlord-owned or shared-ownership structure
  • SAPN embedded-network approval handled

One roof, several businesses, one system that bills fairly.

Strip shops, medical suites, small industrial units. The landlord owns the roof, the tenants own the bills, and nobody wants to be the one who pays for the other. Sixty kilowatts across the whole roof produces around 90,000 kWh a year, more than enough to cover trading-hours load across every tenancy.

Each tenancy is sub-metered so solar is allocated and billed per unit, at a rate below grid. The landlord earns a return on the roof, tenants see a lower bill from day one, and the fifty kilowatt-hours of battery shifts afternoon surplus into the 4 to 7 pm peak when the last tenancies are still open.

We handle the embedded-network application, the lease variation template and the tenant agreements so nobody is guessing who owns what.

New rules that affect your build · 2026

Five rules that shape a commercial build

100 kW STC threshold

Small-scale vs large-scale · federal

Systems up to 100 kW claim STCs, paid up front and applied to the invoice. Systems above 100 kW claim LGCs, an annual generation-based credit paid over the life of the system. The optimal size for most SA commercial rooftops with a three-phase supply is 99 kW, right at the STC ceiling. We will model both if your load supports something bigger.

Small business instant asset write-off

ATO · eligible assets under threshold

For eligible small businesses, the solar system can be written off in the year of install rather than depreciated over its life. This dramatically shortens the after-tax payback period. Threshold and eligibility change with each federal budget · confirm current rules with your accountant before signing.

SAPN commercial connection

SA Power Networks · commercial

Commercial connections above 30 kW require an engineered application and witness inspection. Timeline: 4 to 8 weeks from application to approval. We handle every step, including the network capacity study if required for your suburb. See SAPN flexible export .

NETCC and AS/NZS 3000 commercial

Wiring standards and consumer code

Commercial installs run under the same NETCC framework as residential, plus AS/NZS 3000 wiring compliance and AS/NZS 5033 solar-specific standards. Every install is signed off by a licensed contractor. Our licence: PGE 336477.

Ownership and tenancy structures

Landlord and tenant solar on commercial premises

Solar on a leased commercial roof needs the landlord's permission, because the rebate is tied to the property, not the tenant. We have handled tenant-initiated, landlord-initiated and shared-cost structures, including 50/50 arrangements for shared-benefit commercial solar.
