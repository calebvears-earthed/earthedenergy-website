Outage Backup

That surprises most people. Here is why, and what changes it.

AS 4777 · anti-islanding

It is a safety rule, not a product fault. If the street is dead, a linesman needs to know your roof is not quietly feeding 240 V back up the wire. So every grid-tied inverter watches the grid and disconnects when it disappears. Panels on the roof, blackout inside. Every time.

A battery on its own does not fix this. A battery with backup capability, connected through an automatic transfer switch, does. The inverter isolates the house from the street, forms its own grid, and the panels keep charging the battery through the day.

GoodWe ESA · Sigenergy + Gateway

The GoodWe ESA has the transfer switch built into the unit. Simple, one box, and it carries the whole house up to its rating. The catch is a motor derate of about 30 percent on backup, so a big ducted aircon compressor may not start on battery. Fine for fridges, lights, pumps and most homes.

Sigenergy uses a separate Gateway with its own transfer switch. It moves the house to battery in zero milliseconds, so the microwave clock does not even blink, and there is no derate. Our rule is simple: no Sigenergy without a Gateway. Either you get whole-home backup properly, or you go GoodWe. Full comparison in whole-home backup explained .

It changes the design

If blackouts matter to you, say it in the first ten minutes. It changes the battery, the inverter, the switchboard work and the price. Retrofitting backup onto a system that was not designed for it usually means a new inverter, so it is far cheaper to get right the first time.

Typical outage draw for a Hills home is 1 to 2 kW: fridge, freezer, lights, wifi, a pump, a TV. A 10 kWh battery covers a night. A 20 kWh battery covers a bad weekend. See battery sizing .
