Knowledge · Bushfire prone areas

Bushfire Prone Areas

Component choice, cable routes, isolation and where the battery sits all change with your BAL rating.

AS 3959 · council overlay

BAL ratings run from Low through 12.5, 19, 29, 40 and Flame Zone. Most of the Adelaide Hills sits at BAL 12.5 to 29. Pockets around Cherryville, Montacute and the Kuitpo edge go higher. The rating is about radiant heat and ember attack on the building, and it flows straight through to how we build an electrical system on it.

We pull the overlay before the site visit so we arrive knowing the rating, not guessing it.

In BAL 29 and above we run DC cable in metal conduit rather than plastic, seal every roof penetration with fire-rated collars, and keep the rooftop isolator where CFS crews can reach it from the ground. Every isolator and the main switch get engraved labels, not stickers, because stickers do not survive the first summer.

Batteries go on a non-combustible wall with clearance, or on a free-standing plinth away from the eaves, depending on the rating. Sigenergy and GoodWe both carry the IP and fire ratings for outdoor mounting. We still do not put them under a timber deck.

Bore pumps · fire pumps

SAPN de-energises Hills feeders on catastrophic days and during active fires. If your water comes from a bore or you have a fire pump on a tank, it needs to run with the grid down. That means a backup-wired battery with the pump on the backed-up circuit, and enough capacity to run it for hours, not minutes.

We size this at the site visit. A typical 1.1 kW bore pump running four hours draws about 4.5 kWh. Add the fridge, some lights and a phone charger and a 10 kWh battery covers a long afternoon. See outage backup for how the switchover works.
