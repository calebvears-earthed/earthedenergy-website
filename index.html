How we build the equation

Your bills, your scenario, one number

We start with your previous power bills and the kind of household you run: when you are home, what heats the water, whether an EV is coming. From that we size the solar to your daytime load and the battery to carry the evening, then take the federal STC and Cheaper Home Batteries rebates off before you see a number. A typical Hills scenario is 10 kW of solar with a 25 kWh battery for $16,999 after rebates, or $333 a month over 24 months on finance, which for most $600-a-quarter households sits below the bill it replaces. The daily supply charge, about $100 a quarter, stays. Your written quote lands inside 15 percent of what the calculator tells you.
