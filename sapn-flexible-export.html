SAPN Flexible Export

How often SAPN turns your export down, by how much, and why we still spec a big inverter.

How it works

Export envelope · refreshed every 5 min

A Flexible Exports inverter polls SA Power Networks and receives an export limit for your connection, refreshed every few minutes. Most of the year that number is the full 10 kW per phase. On a mild sunny spring Sunday, when every roof on the feeder is exporting and nobody is home running aircon, it can drop to 1.5 kW or, briefly, to zero.

The throttle applies to export only. Your panels still run your house and charge your battery at full output. Only the surplus going up the wire is curtailed.

SAPN published data

SAPN reports curtailment events cluster in September to November and on public holidays, mainly between 11 am and 2 pm. Across a year that is typically a few dozen hours of reduced export. Against a fixed 1.5 kW limit that curtails every sunny midday, Flexible is not close.

A battery makes the question almost irrelevant. When the export limit drops, surplus goes into the battery instead. GoodWe and Sigenergy both do this automatically.

Why bigger still wins

Say you have a 10 kW inverter and a 13 kW array. At midday in October SAPN drops export to 1.5 kW. The inverter still delivers 3 kW to the house, 5 kW into the battery, and 1.5 kW to the grid. A 5 kW inverter in the same moment would be capped at 5 kW total, and the battery would charge slower.

That is why we do not downsize the inverter to match the export limit. The inverter is sized to the roof and the battery. The export limit is just one output among three.

Figures in these guides are indicative. The price, and any rebate, that applies to your house is the one on the written quote.
