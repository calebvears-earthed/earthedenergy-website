Earthed history

Where Earthed came from, and where Caleb wants it to go

One electrician, twelve years on the grid, and a conviction that renewables win when the numbers are shown plainly.

Caleb on the first Earthed job · wide (Caleb to add)

The person
Caleb Vears · Director

Caleb is a high-voltage electrician first. Before there was a company there was a trade: jointing 33 kV cable on Goyder South Wind Farm near Burra, rebuilding the switch room at Nyrstar Port Pirie after a cable fire, commissioning a 5 MW solar farm at Port Wakefield, and operating the 111 MW battery in the Barossa. That is a decade of watching renewables go from an argument to the backbone of South Australia's grid.

What stayed with him was not the scale. It was how rarely anyone outside the industry got to see how the pieces actually fit together, and how much of the public debate about renewables ran on that gap.

How we started
Earthed High Voltage → Earthed Energy

Earthed High Voltage was set up to do utility work properly: HV connections, BESS, wind and solar farm electrical, with Caleb on the tools and on the drawings. The residential arm came later and almost by accident. People in the Adelaide Hills kept asking Caleb to look over solar quotes from call centres, systems sized from a satellite photo and installed by a subcontractor nobody would ever meet.

Rather than keep saying what was wrong with them, Earthed Energy was started to show what right looked like: the same cable, the same care and the same crew used at 33 kV, scaled down to a family's switchboard. Troy Hooker joined as designer and the two of them still walk every roof.

Early site photo · Goyder South or Port Wakefield (Caleb to add)

The aim
Transparent · open-minded · on the phone

Caleb's view is simple: most people are not against renewables, they are against being sold to. So Earthed publishes its pricing tiers, puts the working behind every saving estimate on the site, and lets you run the quote calculator yourself before anyone calls. If a battery does not pay back for your household, the calculator says so.

Open-mindedness runs the other way too. Earthed does not push one brand or one answer; it explains the trade-offs, listens to how you actually live, and changes the design when it is wrong. The hope is that every home done this way is a small, visible argument for renewables that the neighbours can see working, and that enough of them add up to something bigger than one company.

Show people the numbers, tell them the truth, and the energy transition sells itself.

Caleb Vears
