SA Export Limits

SA Power Networks offers two options. We check which one your address gets, then we size the system.

On this page

  1. What each option means for a 10 kW system

The two options

SAPN · connection agreement

Every new solar connection in South Australia picks one of two export arrangements. The fixed option caps what you send to the grid at 1.5 kW per phase, all day, every day. The Flexible Exports option lets you export up to 10 kW per phase most of the time, and SAPN turns it down towards zero on the handful of afternoons when the local network is saturated.

For a 10 kW system on a 1.5 kW fixed limit, roughly two thirds of your midday production has nowhere to go unless a battery or a hot water load soaks it up. On Flexible Exports the same system exports freely on about 350 days a year. Flexible needs an inverter that talks to SAPN. Every GoodWe and Sigenergy inverter we install does.

How we check

Before the quote

Flexible Exports depends on your transformer and your feeder, not on you. Two houses in Mount Barker on different streets can get different answers. So we check your specific address on the SA Power Networks portal while we are standing in your driveway, and the answer goes onto the quote as a line item.

If your address is fixed-only, the design changes. We lean harder on battery capacity, on shifting hot water and pool loads into the middle of the day, and we are honest that the feed-in credit will be small. See how the throttling actually behaves for the detail.
