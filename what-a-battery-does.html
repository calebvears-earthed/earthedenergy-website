What a Battery Does

Adding the appropriate battery for your situation is the catalyst to $0 power bills.

Why the battery matters

Six things a battery does for an Adelaide home

1

Solar power day and night

Panels stop at sunset. A battery holds the afternoon surplus and runs the house through dinner, the evening and overnight. A 20 kWh battery covers a typical Hills family from 5 pm to 8 am.

2

Less grid reliance

With solar alone most homes still buy 45 percent of their power from the grid. With a right-sized battery that drops to 20 percent or lower, mostly in June and July.

3

Stop exporting for cents

Exported solar earns 3 to 8 cents a kWh. Imported power costs about 42. Storing your own surplus and using it at night is worth five to ten times exporting it.

4

Backup in a blackout

A backup-wired battery keeps fridges, lights, wifi and pumps running when the street goes dark. Sigenergy with Gateway switches in zero milliseconds. GoodWe ESA does it built in. Details here.

5

Lower bills

A $600-a-quarter household with 10 kW of solar and 20 kWh of battery typically lands at $100 to $150 a quarter, most of which is the daily supply charge.

6

Cleaner energy use

Every kWh you shift from evening grid to stored solar displaces gas-fired evening generation. For a Hills home that is roughly 3 to 4 tonnes of CO₂ a year, about what a mid-size car emits.

When it does not stack up
Honest bit

Battery payback depends on how much evening power you have to displace. On a $300 to $400 quarterly bill there is not enough of it, and solar-only pays back in 4 to 5 years while the battery stretches to 12 or more. In that case we quote solar with a battery-ready inverter and you add a battery when it suits your energy consumption. The same applies to commercial clients who only have daytime production.

Above about $600 a quarter, or if blackouts matter to you, the battery earns its place. See battery sizing for the kWh, and how much you save for the payback brackets.
