SWER Lines

If your property is on SWER, tell us early. It moves inverter size, battery size and export before anything else.

What SWER is

Hills · Fleurieu · Mid North · Eyre

A normal street supply brings two or four wires to your house. SWER brings one high-voltage wire and uses the earth itself as the return path, with a small pole transformer stepping down to 240 V at your boundary. It is common past Woodside, through Strathalbyn and Meadows, up the Mid North and across most of Eyre Peninsula.

The transformer is small, usually 10 or 25 kVA shared between a few properties. That is the constraint. It does not care how many panels are on your roof, it cares how much your inverter can push back through it.

The 2025 change

July 2025 · SAPN

Until mid 2025 a SWER property was typically limited to a 5 kVA inverter, which meant about 6.6 kW of panels and very little battery flexibility. SAPN lifted that to 10 kVA for most SWER connections from July 2025, subject to a network check on your specific transformer.

In practice that means a 10 kW GoodWe or Sigenergy inverter with 13 kW of panels is now on the table for many Hills properties that were told no two years ago. If you were quoted small before, it is worth asking again.

Design on SWER

SWER lines are the ones most likely to sit on a fixed 1.5 kW export, and the ones most likely to drop out in a storm. Both point the same way: a battery sized to carry the evening, and backup wiring so the house stays live when the line goes down. See outage backup and bushfire prone areas , because SWER properties are usually both.

We lodge the SAPN pre-approval before you sign anything, so the inverter size on your quote is the size you will actually be allowed to connect.

Figures in these guides are indicative. The price, and any rebate, that applies to your house is the one on the written quote.
